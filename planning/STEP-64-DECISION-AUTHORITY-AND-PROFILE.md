# Step 64: Decision authority and the operating profile

Status: designed 2026-09-12, not yet implemented. This record is the design; the evidence section is filled in at publication.

## Trigger

The founder restated the goal of the framework on 2026-09-12: a harness that a non-technical person can ride, where the owner is asked only about scope, the idea, and the costs that matter to them, and the AI decides architecture, scaffolding, CI, and sequencing by research and recorded planning; secure and cost-efficient at the level the owner wants, from a disposable proof of concept to an enterprise product, with deferrals planned instead of forgotten; efficient with the owner's time and with AI usage. The factory session's gap assessment and five Astra research runs (research/astra-2026-09-12/) agree on what is missing and on what to fix first. This step takes the two foundations every other item stacks on.

## Two findings that decide the order

1. The framework contradicts the founder's operating rule. conventions/19-steering.md says "Get approval before implementing", "Present options before implementing ... wait for a decision", and "The human decides architecture". The enforcer repeats it: "If uncertain, ask", "Never modify existing systems without permission". The founder's engineering constitution says the opposite: the prompter is the CTO, the AI makes every technical decision and states it in one line, escalation is limited to four categories (new recurring spend, external commitments, actions on a live customer environment, a change to what the product is), and the form is one recommendation, never a menu. Three of the five Astra notes flagged this independently. A profile, a ledger, or a budget stacked on the old wording would keep the repeated technical questions the founder wants gone. So the first change is a declared decision-authority setting with the constitution as its default for a non-technical owner.
2. The interview already learns the facts that should set the rigor, then throws them away. Discovery asks "personal project, startup MVP, or enterprise product", who uses it, whether data is regulated, and what the budget is; the answers map to a stack row and nothing else. No record tells a later session how careful to be, what was deliberately deferred, or when a deferral comes due. The debt template has severity and status but no trigger. The validators pass or fail with no way to say "not applicable here" or "deferred until X". So the second change is an operating profile: a stage, the facts that set it, a floor no stage may defer, and trigger-linked deferrals the validators can evaluate.

## Decisions

Each line is a decision of the factory session, with the reason.

1. Two new conventions, not one. #29 Decision Authority & Escalation governs who decides and how the owner is asked. #30 Operating Profile & Deferral governs how careful the project must be and when deferred work comes due. Different concerns, different readers, routed separately from the index (the framework's rule: many focused docs, correctly routed).
2. Decision authority is a declared project setting with two values: `owner-decides` and `ai-decides`, recorded with its provenance (`Authority source: owner-stated` or `defaulted`). The escalation list applies under both; the values differ only in whether technical plans wait for approval. Default when a new project's owner answers vaguely: `ai-decides`, marked `defaulted`, because the owner keeps every escalation category either way and delivery does not stall. An existing project being migrated defaults the other way, to `owner-decides`, because its team worked under the old approval rules and nobody asked to change that. Moving from `owner-decides` to `ai-decides` requires an owner statement recorded as the provenance; an AI-authored log line is not one.
3. Convention 19 is reconciled, not deleted. Its discipline (specification before complex features, plan before multi-file changes, one change at a time, no adjacent refactors, no phantom requirements, fresh start after two failed corrections) is authority-independent and stays. Its five authority-dependent rules become conditional on the setting. Under `ai-decides`, "get approval" becomes "record the plan and proceed", "present options and wait" becomes "choose, record the alternatives rejected, proceed", "stop and ask when uncertain" splits into "ask about scope and product intent; research, decide, and record about technical ambiguity", "never modify foundational systems without permission" becomes "without a recorded decision", and "the human decides architecture" becomes "the owner decides what the product is, who it serves, what it promises, what it may spend and commit; the AI decides architecture and records it". The breaking-change protocol keeps its phased shape and drops the wait under `ai-decides` unless the change touches a live customer environment, which is an escalation category under any setting.
4. The enforcer carries the constitution. CLAUDE.md gets new rules: beyond the approvals `owner-decides` asks for, escalate only the five categories (new recurring spend; external commitments; actions on a live customer environment; irreversible destruction of valuable data, access, or secrets; a change to what the product is) and always as one plain recommendation; never claim something works, exists, is deployed, is green, or was sent without verifying it in this session; never weaken, skip, or placeholder a gate to finish; record every consequential technical decision (one that introduces or changes a dependency, a boundary, an operational obligation, a meaningful cost, or a deliberate compromise) with its reason at the project's decision location, while routine implementation follows the governing decision without a new record. Two existing rules change: "If uncertain, ask" becomes scope-or-intent only; "Never modify existing systems without permission" becomes "without a recorded decision (permission under owner-decides)". The fifth category is the founder's standing confirm-first rule for irreversible actions, added to the constitution's four; the design review asked for the reconciliation.
5. The owner-facing safety rails are part of #29, in plain words: the AI never, without the owner's specific or clearly bounded standing authorization, starts paid services or recurring commitments or spends past the approved allowance, sends anything to customers or publishes or signs or promises on the owner's behalf, changes a live customer system including pushing or merging into a branch that deploys, permanently erases valuable data or changes live access and secrets in ways that lock people out, or changes what the product does or who it serves; and the AI never exposes secrets, invents evidence, bypasses independent review, or weakens a gate to make failing work look finished. Silence is not authorization.
6. The profile is a project-owned markdown file at the project root, `PROFILE.md`, with `- Key: value` bullets, the same parse contract as References.md, so the shipped bash and awk validators read it without a new dependency. One file per project, even when a fullstack project has two References.md files, because stage and authority are project-wide. The template ships as templates/profile.md.
7. The stage model is Astra's R1 model: `isolated` (private experiment, synthetic disposable data, no real external effects, no operational reliance), `trial` (controlled evaluation with identified participants and a workable fallback), `operational` (people depend on the result, records, availability, or real transactions). The factory session dropped its own "0 to 100 percent compromise" dial: missing authentication cannot be offset by excellent documentation, so a percentage has no denominator. The stage is derived by the AI from recorded facts; the owner never picks a label.
8. The facts are recorded next to the stage, each with an explicit `unknown` allowed and never read as "no": audience (owner-only, identified-participants, public), data (synthetic-disposable, real-nonpersonal, personal), external effects (money, messages, records, health-safety-access), operational reliance, valuable records, fallback, contributors, regulated data, customer commitments, monthly running-cost ceiling, AI spend envelope, decision authority with its source, observed-on date, and review condition. The stage key is `Operating stage`, so it cannot be confused with the deployment `Stage` in References.md. Keys are exact and occur once; a duplicate, an unknown key, an empty value, or a template placeholder fails. The stage must be consistent with the facts: `isolated` requires owner-only audience, synthetic disposable data, no external effects, no reliance, no valuable records, no commitments; `trial` requires a non-public audience, no operational reliance, and a fallback not recorded as `no`; regulated data with synthetic-only data is a contradiction. A budget field confers no spending authority.
9. The floor: six obligations no stage may defer whenever the protected asset or hazardous capability exists. Secrets and environment protection; validation of untrusted input at every boundary, public endpoints included, and authorization wherever access is restricted; safe irreversible effects (recovery, confirmation, reconciliation, or duplicate prevention as appropriate for valuable records and external actions); responsible personal-data handling before collection (purpose, access, retention, and the applicable deletion and export procedure, retention exceptions respected, a verified manual procedure acceptable where obligations and volume permit); authorized reuse of code, assets, and data with notices preserved; honest completion and review (verify shipped behavior, keep required checks passing, independent audit before merge wherever a merge path exists, no relabeling of failing or placeholder work). The controls a project owes are the union of the floor, the stage's baseline, the requirements its facts add, and its existing commitments; the floor is not an exhaustive checklist. A capability the project lacks is recorded as justified non-applicability, never as a deferral. A deferral whose control is labeled a floor item is invalid; the validator fails it. The validator can only see the label: whether a `#23` deferral is rate limiting (deferrable) or input validation (floor) is a judgment the independent audit makes, and the record says so.
10. Deferrals live in TECHNICAL-DEBT.md, not in a new ledger. The template gains strictly parseable fields: `Kind` (shortcut or deferral), `Control` (the convention, backend rule, or floor item concerned), `Due-before` (a named trigger or a date), `Review-by` (a date), `Closure-evidence` (what proves it closed). Existing entries keep validating; the new fields are required only on entries marked `Kind: deferral`.
11. Named triggers, evaluated against the profile's facts: `first-outside-participant`, `public-access`, `real-data`, `personal-data`, `real-money-or-external-action`, `operational-reliance`, `valuable-records`, `second-contributor`, `regulated-data-or-commitment`, `trial-stage`, `operational-stage`. (The design first had a single `stage-promotion` trigger; a single file cannot tell which promotion a deferral meant, so the two stage triggers replace it. `regulated-claim-or-commitment` became `regulated-data-or-commitment` because the profile records regulated data and customer commitments, not claims.) A date deadline is inclusive: the deferral is blocking on the due date. A trigger resting on an `unknown` fact is `UNVERIFIED`. A trigger is true when the recorded facts say so; a true trigger makes every deferral due before it blocking. Relabeling the stage, renewing the date, or marking won't-fix does not clear a triggered deferral; downgrading a stage requires a recorded reason and a review of surviving obligations. The facts are declared by the project, so detection is as good as the declaration; the validator says so in its output rather than implying observation it does not have.
12. One new project validator, scripts/validate-profile.sh, a standalone gate named in DEVELOP.md Step 6 and MAINTAIN.md Mode 1 rather than chained inside the other validators, so each script keeps doing one thing and a run is never counted twice. It reports, per check, one of OK, FAIL, WARN, DEFERRED, or UNVERIFIED, the last for a fact recorded as unknown or a trigger resting on one, and it prints where the profile came from (`declared` or `missing`). Two modes. In the default mode an absent PROFILE.md is a WARN read as `operational` with every fact unknown, so an installed project without deferrals keeps passing after the update and is told what to add; but a deferral that cannot be evaluated because the profile is missing is FAIL, so never creating the file is not a way around the deferrals. In `--strict` mode, run before any action that changes exposure and in maintenance of operational projects, every UNVERIFIED result is an error too. Malformed or duplicate keys, invalid values, placeholders, stage-versus-facts contradictions, floor deferrals, triggered deferrals, reached due dates, and deferral entries missing required fields are FAIL. A past `Review-by` is WARN. NOT_APPLICABLE is not in this validator's vocabulary: it has no per-convention applicability to evaluate yet, and saying so is better than inventing the word; the declaration-driven gates are a later step. The validator checks declarations and their consistency; it does not observe users, data, or money, cannot tell whether a stated fact is true, has no memory of earlier profiles (a downgrade that also rewrites the facts is caught by review, not by the script), and does not stand at any action boundary. The convention tells the AI when to run it; the playbooks name it; nothing here claims automatic prevention.
13. The interview gains the questions that set the profile, in plain words, asked only when not already answered: what the first version must prove or let someone accomplish; whether it uses made-up or real information and about whom; whether it could move money, send messages, change important records, or affect someone's health, safety, work, or access; what happens if it stops working or is wrong and whether people have another way; whether this is an idea that may be discarded or something people will depend on, and whether there is a promised date; what was promised to the people involved about privacy and reliability; what monthly running cost and additional AI spending is comfortable; and whether the owner wants to make technical choices themself or have the AI decide and show what it chose and why. Vague answers keep `unknown`. For audience, effects, reliance, and records, `unknown` blocks real exposure and leaves the contained synthetic-data experiment open. The regulated-data question keeps its existing stricter gate: unresolved, it halts scaffolding and deployment as the onboarding and scaffold rules already say, because a wrong "no" there is the costliest error. The two rules are stated side by side so they cannot be read as one.
14. Bootstrap generates PROFILE.md in Step 4 from the answers, derives the stage, records the decision-authority setting, and logs the profile in the Step 6 bootstrap entry. Existing-project migration generates it from the audit with facts marked unknown where the audit cannot tell.
15. The operating stage also sets what bootstrap and scaffold may defer, following R1's table: at `isolated`, detailed research for unused capabilities, remote deployment, capacity engineering, operational dashboards, and future integrations may be deferred; purpose, success criteria, operating boundary, containment, protected configuration, reproducible verification, and an integrated smoke test may not. Every deferral gets a debt entry with a trigger. "Later" without a trigger is not a deferral, it is a loose end, and the validator treats an entry without `Due-before` as malformed.
16. Routing: Conventions.md indexes #29 and #30 and adds lookup rows ("Anything that might need the owner", "Deferring work or taking a shortcut", "Starting a new AI session" adds #29). CLAUDE.md's Before Any Work reads PROFILE.md with References.md. DEVELOP.md Step 3 and MAINTAIN.md Mode 1 and the debt pruning policy reference the setting and the validator. RED-FLAGS.md gains two rows: a vague answer about who decides, and a stage downgrade while exposure exists.
17. Out of scope for this step, recorded for the roadmap and not parked without a trigger: compact decision records with review conditions, the research protocol, correction capture and factory intake (Step 65); the AI economy playbook, starters, usage reporting (Step 66); the unattended window (Step 67); proof of gate, cost ledger, exit paths, license (Step 68). Each has a design input in research/astra-2026-09-12/ and its trigger is this step's publication.

## Design review (Astra, 2026-09-12, read-only, FIX-FIRST)

The design record was reviewed adversarially by the founder's Codex CLI (GPT-6 Astra) before implementation. Ten findings; dispositions:

- Departures from the research not acknowledged (decision 4 recorded every decision where the research said consequential ones; decision 12 had no NOT_APPLICABLE and no action semantics; decision 13 contradicted the existing scaffold halts): fixed in decisions 4, 12, 13 above.
- Gate-sensitive wording (counts, the README id range, factory language): counts removed from the product, range updated, no factory language shipped.
- Profile contract too loose (duplicates accepted, stage key collides with the deployment stage, data recorded as one yes/no, no fallback or valuable-records facts, no authority provenance): decision 8 now carries the key set; `Operating stage`, `Data`, `Fallback`, `Valuable records`, `Authority source` added. Policy revision, scope, evidence references, baseline references, and budget authorization references were proposed too and are deferred to the decision-record step, where evidence lives; a budget field confers no authority, stated in the convention.
- Triggers: `stage-promotion` unevaluable, regulated claim without a fact, valuable records missing, deadline semantics undefined: decision 11 rewritten.
- Floor qualifiers dropped: restored in decision 9, with the governing union and justified non-applicability.
- Default `ai-decides` as self-authorization: decision 2 now records provenance, defaults migrated projects to `owner-decides`, and requires an owner statement to move to `ai-decides`; the escalation list gains irreversible destruction (decision 4).
- Missing-profile escape: decision 12 now fails deferrals that cannot be evaluated without a profile and adds `--strict`.
- Fixture matrix: the verification plan below lists the expanded set.
- Enforcement beyond the machinery: decision 12 states what the script cannot do; decision 9 states the floor-labeling limit; decision 15 defines how a deferral satisfies scaffold completion.
- Owner-decides workflow: convention 19 keeps every approval under that setting and the enforcer's escalation rule is phrased "beyond the approvals owner-decides asks for".

- test-entrypoints.py: all tests pass against the legacy export.
- test-profile.py: valid profiles at every stage and under both authority settings pass with exit 0 and no FAIL; every planted violation fails with its expected message: unknown, duplicate, missing, empty, and placeholder keys; every invalid enumeration; malformed dates; every row of the contradiction table; regulated data with synthetic-only data; each floor item as a deferral; every trigger in its true, false, and unknown state; a reached and a future due date; a deferral missing each required field; an unknown trigger name; won't-fix on a triggered deferral; fixed entries ignored; legacy entries without Kind untouched; entries inside fenced examples ignored; an absent profile with no deferrals (WARN, exit 0), with a deferral (FAIL), and in strict mode; strict mode turning UNVERIFIED into an error.
